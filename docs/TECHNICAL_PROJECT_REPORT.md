## 1. Project overview

### 1.1 Industrial problem

Automated visual inspection of manufactured surfaces is a supervised segmentation
problem that is chronically starved of positive examples. In KolektorSDD2 (KSDD2),
the dataset used here, only **356 of 3,335** images contain a defect, and the
partition available for model development contains just **209** defective images
with pixel-level masks (`reports/data_audit/summary.json`,
`reports/gan_inputs/summary.json`). Defects are rare, geometrically diverse,
expensive to capture on a production line, and expensive to annotate at pixel
level.

### 1.2 Motivation for synthetic defect generation

The standard proposed remedy is generative augmentation: if a model can synthesize
new defective images, the detector sees more positive examples and should
generalize better. This is a widely published strategy and is usually evaluated by
comparing a synthetic-augmented detector against a plain baseline.

This project's premise is that such a comparison is **not sufficient**. A synthetic
sample is defective by construction, so injecting synthetic data into a
class-balanced batch silently raises the fraction of defective samples the detector
sees. Any measured gain is then attributable either to the synthetic *content* or
merely to the changed *class prevalence*, and the usual two-arm design cannot
separate them. The project was therefore built to answer the harder, controlled
question.

### 1.3 Intended users and use case

Research and engineering use only. The deliverable is a reproducible experimental
system plus its recorded findings. **There is no deployment, no serving component,
no factory integration, and no production inference path** anywhere in the
repository. The only inference-facing utility is a read-only single-image
inspection script (`scripts/demo_segment_image.py`) added at release time for
reviewers.

### 1.4 Final project objective

> Determine whether synthetic defects generated by a mask-conditioned residual GAN
> provide downstream segmentation utility **beyond what an equivalent amount of
> real-defect exposure already provides**, under a protocol frozen before training.

### 1.5 Overall system pipeline

```
KSDD2 official train split (2,331 images)
  → audited extraction + deterministic development split (manifest-only, seed 42)
  → defect template / mask extraction        (development-training images only)
  → deterministic placement onto normal backgrounds (compatibility-indexed)
  → mask-conditioned residual GAN            (local refinement of the composite)
  → frozen GAN checkpoint → 512 materialized synthetic samples
  → segmentation detector training           (equal budget, identical init)
  → prevalence-matched controlled evaluation → precommitted 8-criterion gate
```

The official KSDD2 held-out test split (1,004 images) was **never constructed,
inspected, counted, or evaluated** at any point. `official_test_access_count` is
`0` and `official_test_samples_loaded` is `0` in every recorded report, and
`reports/g2_2/official_test/` does not exist.

### 2.4 Image geometry and the model canvas

KSDD2 images are tall, narrow strips. Native widths in development-training normal
images range **184–241** px (`reports/gan_input_design/normal_valid_fraction_audit.json`);
heights are approximately 630. The measured maximum across the dataset is
**241 × 665**, rounded up to the next multiple of 32 to give a fixed model canvas
of **256 × 672** (`src/defectgen/data/full_image.py`: `MODEL_WIDTH = 256`,
`MODEL_HEIGHT = 672`; rationale in `docs/design-decisions.md`).

Images are **never resized or aspect-distorted**. Instead:

- **Image padding:** reflection (`reflection_pad`, `pad_full_sample`).
- **Mask padding:** constant **zero**, never reflection — reflecting a border
  defect would manufacture false positive labels at the edge.
- **`valid_region` tensor:** 1 over original pixels, 0 over padding. All losses and
  all metrics are computed on valid pixels only.
- Original dimensions and per-side offsets travel with each sample, so predictions
  restore exactly to native size via `restore_to_native`.

### 2.5 Normalization, sampling, augmentation

- **Normalization:** per-channel mean `[0.17537, 0.17091, 0.17704]`, std
  `[0.04440, 0.03818, 0.03754]`, computed from **development-train original pixels
  only** (`configs/final_real_baseline_bf16.json → data.detector_normalization`).
  Inputs are scaled to `[0,1]` then standardized.
- **Sampling:** `deterministic_weighted` with `target_defective_fraction: 0.5` and
  `replacement: true` — a `WeightedRandomSampler`-equivalent law that balances the
  otherwise ~10% defective class.
- **Augmentation:** synchronized horizontal and vertical flips, p=0.5 each, applied
  jointly to `image`, `mask`, and `valid_region`, with `mask_interpolation: "none"`
  to keep masks strictly binary. Training only. No rotation, colour jitter, CutMix,
  or MixUp — deliberately excluded pending a documented experiment
  (`docs/design-decisions.md`).
- **Loader contract:** every loader consumes the validated manifest. Directory
  globbing is explicitly not an acceptable sample-discovery mechanism.

### 2.6 Preprocessing defects discovered and corrected

Four real bugs were found and fixed in the GAN input pipeline. These are genuine
engineering findings, each with a recorded audit.

| Phase | Defect found | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| **F1.1** | Normal-background eligibility used a hard `0.9` valid-fraction threshold, which silently **biased background selection toward wider images**. | Threshold set to the audited native-valid fraction **0.71875**, removing the width bias while keeping all reflected padding ineligible for defect placement. Audit: `reports/gan_input_design/normal_valid_fraction_audit.json` (1,772 images). |
| **F1.2** | Border-touching defects lost their **censoring semantics** when transformed — a defect that genuinely touched an image edge could be placed away from any edge, fabricating an unphysical geometry. | Explicit contact sides are transformed with the flips and must match target native edges; non-border defects retain an 8-pixel native margin. Adds `gan_manifest_content_sha256` and category-aware visual audits. |
| **F1.3** | Placement used background-by-background **retry loops**, which was slow and conflated "impossible geometry" with "unlucky draw". | Replaced with a deterministic native-geometry **compatibility index**. Index exclusions are now counted separately from real placement retries. |
| **F1.4** | Reflection padding was centred on the tensor rather than the **native window**, and placement was validated against tensor edges instead of inclusive native-valid bounds. This produced asymmetric left/right sampling and impossible-state retries. | Padding centred on the narrow native window; feasible flip and rounded-scale intervals indexed *before* sampling. |

| Empty compatibility pools | 243 | **0** |

## 3. Real-data defect detector baseline

This is the **segmentation detector** — distinct from the GAN generator and
discriminator, and distinct from the downstream augmentation experiment.

### 3.1 Architecture

`src/defectgen/models/unet.py` — a GroupNorm U-Net.

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Input | `[B, 3, 672, 256]` normalized RGB on the padded canvas |
| Output | `[B, 1, 672, 256]` raw **logits**, no sigmoid |
| Base channels | 32 |
| Downsampling | learned stride-2 convolution |
| Decoder | bilinear resize followed by convolution |
| Normalization | GroupNorm |
| **Parameters** | **28,285,569** (verified empirically; matches `model_parameter_count` in `reports/final_real_baseline_bf16_seed42/summary.json`) |

### 3.2 Loss

`valid_region_bce_with_logits_plus_soft_dice`:

- BCE-with-logits with `pos_weight = 5.0`, compensating the strong negative-pixel
  imbalance.
- Soft Dice.
- Weights 1.0 / 1.0, accumulated in **float32** regardless of autocast precision.
- Computed over **valid-region pixels only** — padded pixels never contribute.

|---|---|

### 3.6 Failed and diagnostic runs

These are retained deliberately and are part of the engineering record.

**E1 — FP16 baseline failure** (`docs/failed-fp16-baseline.md`,
`configs/final_real_baseline.json`, `reports/final_real_baseline/failure_summary.json`).
Configured with AdamW LR `1e-3`, FP16 autocast with GradScaler, and **no gradient
clipping**. It completed three epochs and failed during epoch-4 validation. Epoch 1
batch 338 and epoch 3 batch 14 each produced an **infinite unscaled gradient norm**;
in both cases forward losses and logits were finite, the update was skipped, and
GradScaler reduced its scale.

Diagnosis: CPU inspection found every model and optimizer-state tensor finite, and a
read-only precision diagnostic re-ran both persisted checkpoints under FP16, BF16,
and FP32 with finite logits and unchanged parameter hashes. This **located the
failure in the unsaved epoch-4 optimization state, not in either checkpoint**. The
run was not patched into passing — it was retired and replaced.

**Fixes carried into E1.2:** BF16 instead of FP16 (wider exponent range, no loss
scaling needed), LR reduced `1e-3 → 3e-4`, gradient clipping added at max norm 1.0,
GradScaler removed, and automatic fp32 retry disabled so a numerical failure is
loud rather than silently absorbed.

**Class-weight pilot** (`configs/loss_pilot.json`, `reports/loss_pilot/`) — a
provisional sweep over BCE `pos_weight ∈ {1, 5, 10, 20}` with per-candidate
threshold sweeps, plus corrected mixed-precision step accounting
(`docs/numerical-stability.md`). This is what selected `pos_weight = 5`.

## 4. Synthetic defect generation pipeline

Configuration: `configs/gan_inputs.json` (`pipeline_version: f1_4_gan_inputs_v1`).
Implementation: `src/defectgen/gan/`. Contract: `docs/gan-input-pipeline.md`.

### 4.1 Source pairing

- **Defect templates** are extracted from the 209 defective **development-training**
  images only → 232 accepted components (§2.3).
- **Normal backgrounds** are drawn from the 1,772 normal **development-training**
  images, subject to `minimum_normal_valid_fraction = 0.71875`. All 1,772 were
  accepted; background patch availability is 3,544.
- Development **validation** and official **test** rows are hard-rejected by the
  loader. `validation_rows_loaded: 0` and `official_test_rows_loaded: 0` in every
  audit.

The working patch is **256 × 512** (`configs/gan_inputs.json → patch`), distinct
from the detector's 256 × 672 full-image canvas.

### 4.2 Placement and compositing

1. A template is sampled, then transformed: horizontal flip p=0.5, vertical flip
   p=0.5, scale in `[0.9, 1.1]`, with `minimum_retained_area_fraction = 0.75`.
2. A **deterministic compatibility index** (F1.3/F1.4) precomputes which
   (background, flip, rounded-scale) combinations are geometrically feasible, so
   sampling never enters an impossible state. On the F1.4 audit this eliminated all
   243 empty pools and all 230 retries.
3. Border semantics are preserved: a template whose source defect touched specific
   native edges must be placed touching the corresponding transformed edges;
   non-border defects keep an 8-pixel native margin
   (`template_transform.non_border_native_margin`).
4. **Colour matching** is applied at the seam — boundary radius 7, gain clamped to
   `[0.8, 1.2]`, absolute offset ≤ 0.15 — so the pasted defect is photometrically
   compatible with its new background before the GAN ever sees it.
5. A feathered alpha (`feather_radius = 5`) blends the composite.

Each sample yields a **coarse composite image, a binary defect mask, and a
`valid_region`** — the GAN's inputs.

### 4.3 Validity, containment, and locality guarantees

These are enforced in code and verified in audits, not assumed:

- **Containment:** `support_pixels_outside_valid_region = 0` in both the F1.3 and
  F1.4 audits and in the G2.3A hard check over all 512 materialized masks.
- **No accidental contact:** `accidental_contact_violations = 0` — a non-border
  defect never accidentally lands on an edge.
- **Binary masks:** masks stay strictly binary; derived dilated/feathered masks are
  stored separately and never overwrite ground truth.
- **Minimum content:** `minimum_positive_pixels = 8`,
  `minimum_component_coverage = 0.05`.
- **Provenance:** every synthetic row records its template and background source
  identity. G2.3A verified **617 distinct template/background source identities,
  all in development-train**, with detector-validation overlap **0**.

### 4.4 Support region and preservation of unaffected pixels

The generator does not regenerate the image. It computes a **support mask** =
the binary defect mask dilated by `support_dilation_radius = 12`, via a max-pool
of kernel `2r+1` (`MaskedResidualGenerator._support_mask`). The refinement is then
applied as:

```python
refined = torch.where(expanded_support, composite + directional_residual, composite)
```

Outside the dilated support the output is the composite **bit-for-bit**, by
construction rather than by penalty. `locality_violations = 0` across the entire
2,000-update sustained run (`reports/gan_training/g2_1_2000/summary.json`).

## 5. GAN architecture

Definition: `src/defectgen/models/gan.py`.
Config: `configs/gan_architecture.json`
(`architecture_version: g1_5a_identity_range_aware_residual_gan_v1`).
Design rationale: `docs/gan-architecture.md`.

**Verified parameter counts** (computed by instantiating the models from the
tracked config):

| Model | Parameters |
|---|---:|
| `MaskedResidualGenerator` | **5,896,739** |
| `MaskConditionedPatchDiscriminator` | **695,649** |
| GAN total | **6,592,388** |
| *(Detector U-Net, for contrast)* | *28,285,569* |

### 5.1 Generator — `MaskedResidualGenerator`

A U-Net residual refiner operating on 256 × 512 patches.

- **Input:** 4 channels — the composite RGB in `[-1, 1]` concatenated with the
  binary defect mask (this is the **mask conditioning**).
- **Stem:** ReflectionPad(3) → 7×7 Conv(4 → 32, bias-free) → GroupNorm(8) → SiLU.
- **Encoder:** exactly 3 downsample stages (asserted in `validate()`), widths
  32 → 64 → 128 → 256, each ReflectionPad(1) → 3×3 stride-2 Conv → GroupNorm → SiLU.
- **Bottleneck:** 4 residual blocks at 256 channels, each two 3×3 convs with
  GroupNorm and SiLU plus an identity skip.
- **Decoder:** 3 upsample-skip blocks using bilinear `F.interpolate` to the skip's
  spatial size, then channel concatenation and two 3×3 convs.
- **Output head:** ReflectionPad(3) → 7×7 Conv(32 → 3), **weights and bias both
  zero-initialized**.
- Normalization is GroupNorm throughout (8 groups), never BatchNorm — appropriate
  for batch size 2.

**Residual formulation and output-range handling.** The zero-initialized head means
the raw residual is exactly zero at initialization, so **the generator starts as an
exact identity function**. The raw residual is then mapped through
`range_aware_residual`:

```python
direction    = torch.tanh(raw_residual)                       # bounded [-1, 1]
positive_cap = torch.minimum(residual_scale, 1.0 - image)     # headroom to +1
negative_cap = torch.minimum(residual_scale, image + 1.0)     # headroom to -1
residual     = where(direction >= 0, direction * positive_cap,
                                      direction * negative_cap)
```

with `residual_scale = 0.25`. This is a **directional, range-aware** formulation:
the residual is bounded by the configured maximum magnitude *and* by the pixel's
actual remaining distance to the valid range boundary, so
`composite + residual` can never leave `[-1, 1]`. Range violations become
structurally impossible rather than clipped after the fact —
`output_range_violations = 0` across the full sustained run.

A persistent `_residual_semantics_marker` buffer is baked into the state dict so
checkpoints written under older residual semantics cannot be silently resumed.

### 5.2 Discriminator — `MaskConditionedPatchDiscriminator`

A conditional PatchGAN returning **raw patch logits** (no sigmoid).

- **Input:** 4 channels — image concatenated with the canonical defect mask.
- 4×4 convolutions with widths 32 → 64 → 128 → 256, strides 2, 2, 2, 1, then a
  final 4×4 conv to 1 channel.
- **Spectral normalization on every convolution**
  (`torch.nn.utils.parametrizations.spectral_norm`); the config validator *refuses*
  to build a discriminator without it.
- LeakyReLU(0.2) activations; GroupNorm(8) on the interior blocks.
- Minimum input size 24 × 24, enforced.

### 5.3 Architectural changes made during debugging

- **G1.5a — identity/range-aware output head.** The original output head did not
  start at identity and produced values outside `[-1, 1]` that had to be clamped.
  Clamping saturated during the very first joint update (§7.4). The fix was the
  zero-initialized head plus `range_aware_residual`, which changed the failure mode
  from "clamp after the fact" to "cannot exceed range by construction". The
  architecture version string and the state-dict marker were both bumped so old
  checkpoints are rejected rather than silently reinterpreted.
- **G1.3b — discriminator view alignment.** Real and fake branches are constructed
  on the *intersection* of real and fake native validity; both are exactly zero
  outside that intersection, while inside it original pixels and fake-branch
  gradients are preserved. Without this, the discriminator could separate real from
  fake using validity-region differences rather than defect appearance.

## 6. GAN losses

Implementation: `src/defectgen/training/gan_losses.py`.
Config: `configs/gan_losses.json` (`loss_version: g1_2_localized_gan_losses_v1`).
Definitions: `docs/gan-losses.md`.

A shared **canonical mask policy** (`canonicalize_discriminator_mask`, threshold
0.5) is applied to both real and fake conditioning so the discriminator cannot
exploit a formatting difference between branches.

**Localization.** `patch_logit_localization_weights` projects the dilated mask into
the discriminator's patch-logit grid using `localization_radius = 35`, and every
adversarial term is a `localized_weighted_mean` over that projection. Without this,
the overwhelming majority of PatchGAN outputs correspond to untouched background
and would dominate the loss.

| Loss | Formula (as implemented) | Why it exists |
|---|---|---|
| **Discriminator hinge** | `mean_w(relu(1 − real_logits)) + mean_w(relu(1 + fake_logits))` | Standard hinge GAN objective on raw logits, restricted to defect-local patches. Callers must pass `fake.detach()`. |
| **Generator adversarial** | `−mean_w(fake_logits)` | Drives the refined composite toward the real-defect distribution, again defect-local. |
| **Change (magnitude) loss** | mean absolute `refined − composite` inside support | A **magnitude regularizer, not a paired reconstruction loss** — there is no ground-truth refined target. It prevents the generator from making arbitrarily large edits and anchors it near the composite. The docstring states this explicitly. |
| **Boundary seam loss** | mean absolute difference over the **inner boundary ring** of the support | Suppresses a visible seam where the refined region meets the untouched background. The ring is `support ∧ dilate(¬support, width)` with `boundary_ring_width = 3`. |
| **Masked total variation** | mean absolute first differences of the residual, counted only across pixel pairs **both inside** support | Discourages high-frequency speckle in the residual without penalizing the genuine step at the support boundary. |
| **Localized R1 gradient penalty** | squared gradient norm of the localized real score w.r.t. the real image | Discriminator regularization for stability. Applied **lazily** every 16 steps with `gamma = 1.0` and the conventional `gamma * interval / 2` rescaling. |

**Identity / outside-mask constraint.** There is deliberately **no** outside-mask
penalty loss. Preservation of unaffected pixels is a *hard architectural guarantee*
via `torch.where` (§4.4), which is strictly stronger than a soft penalty — it makes
outside-support change exactly zero rather than merely small.

**Aggregation weights** used in the final sustained run
(`configs/gan_training_2000.json → generator_loss_weights`):

```
adversarial 1.0 | change 1.0 | boundary 1.0 | total_variation 0.1
```

The TV weight was lowered from the architecture-audit default of 1.0 to 0.1 for
sustained training. `configs/gan_losses.json` marks its own weights
`aggregation_weights_provisional: true`.

**Numerical guards inside the losses.** Every loss validates finiteness, rank,
channel count, and mask range, and every normalized loss guards a zero denominator
by returning a correctly-shaped zero that still participates in autograd
(`residual.sum() * 0`) rather than producing NaN.

## 7. GAN training and stability work

Trainer: `src/defectgen/training/gan_trainer.py`, `gan_smoke.py`.
Mechanics: `docs/gan-training-mechanics.md`, `docs/gan-smoke.md`.

The GAN was **not** trained by launching a long run and hoping. It was brought up
through four escalating gated stages, each of which could and sometimes did stop
the run.

### 7.1 G1.4 — bounded mechanics harness

`scripts/audit_gan_one_step.py`. Eight deterministic no-update **calibration**
batches measure raw loss and unit-gradient scales, then exactly **one** isolated
discriminator update and **one** isolated generator update are executed. It writes
no checkpoint. This established the lazy-R1 convention
(`(global_step + 1) % 16 == 0`) and the finite-gradient, parameter-isolation, and
exact-locality checks reused by every later stage.

### 7.2 Discriminator warmup

Ten discriminator-only updates precede any joint step. The fixed monitor batch is
evaluated at steps 0, 5, and 10, and the run proceeds **only** if mean real logit
exceeds mean fake logit. A non-positive margin extends warmup to at most 20 steps;
it never triggers an automatic learning-rate change. In the preserved run the
margins were `0.00801`, `0.02934`, `0.02096` — accepted.

### 7.3 Staged smoke gating

Warmup → **20-step micro-smoke** → 200-step smoke. Only a clean micro-smoke may
continue. Every update retains G1.3b aligned discriminator views and the G1.4
mechanics checks.

### 7.4 The clamp-saturation failure — a gate that actually fired

The first G1.5 run **stopped itself immediately after the first joint D/G update**
because the generator clamp-saturation fraction was `0.07349`, above the mandatory
`0.05` limit (`docs/gan-smoke.md`).

This was verified as genuine, not a spurious gate: a read-only check against the
pre-clamp candidate confirmed **1,554 of 21,201** support-channel pixels
(`0.07330`) exceeded `[-1, 1]`, while the corresponding composite support contained
**no** already-saturated pixels. So the generator itself was pushing pixels out of
range on its very first update.

The stopped update nevertheless showed correct mechanics: 100% canonical
adversarial-gradient coverage, zero invalid-region adversarial gradient, zero
outside-support change, support mean change `0.13644`.

**Fix:** the G1.5a identity/range-aware output head (§5.3). This is the change that
made range violations structurally impossible.

### 7.5 G1.6 — discriminator clipping ablation and a false-positive gate

`reports/gan_training/g1_6_dclip10_ablation/`, `docs/gan-g1-6-ablation.md`,
`docs/gan-g1-6-selection.md`.

Exactly one controlled change was tested: discriminator gradient clip `5 → 10` and
discriminator LR `5e-5 → 2.5e-5`. Everything else — generator settings, data
identities, seed, BF16, warmup, losses, smoke limits — held fixed.

| Measure | Baseline (G1.5b) | D-clip-10 candidate |
|---|---:|---:|
| Discriminator clipping, overall | 70.48% | **30.92%** |
| Discriminator clipping, final 60 joint steps | 98.33% | **46.67%** |
| Final-60 mean real−fake margin | 0.09690 | 0.05644 |
| Detector-statistic L2 distance from genuine real (28-sample audit) | 0.22863 | **0.15758** |

The candidate initially **stopped at joint step 197** on the canonical-gradient
safety check. Investigation found this was a **gate bug**, not a model failure: the
old gate counted nonzero RGB *scalar components*, when it should count finite,
nonzero RGB *vectors per canonical pixel*. After the gate was corrected, the same
checkpoint resumed deterministically and completed **200/200** joint updates with
zero output-range, locality, invalid-gradient, and non-finite canonical-gradient
violations.

Four **blinded** comparison sheets, each covering four unique samples from seven
geometry/morphology strata, were reviewed before reveal. The baseline was preferred
— the candidate more often produced an edge-dominant, ring-like residual.

**Selection outcome:** D-clip-10 was selected for the sustained run because it
completed the safety contract while materially reducing clipping. This was recorded
explicitly as *a configuration selection, not a claim that any inspected sample is a
best model*. Both smoke runs and their checkpoints are preserved unchanged.

### 10.3 G2.3B — the corrected experiment

Protocol frozen **before any G2.3B detector was trained**, at commit `7a069a5`.
Config: `configs/g2_3b_utility_confirmation.json`. Plan:
`reports/g2_3b/plan/precommitted_plan.json`. Docs:
`docs/g2-3b-utility-protocol.md`, `docs/g2-3b-results.md`.

**Hypothesis.** Does frozen GAN checkpoint 1,500 provide detector utility *beyond
the effect of increasing defective-sample prevalence*, when detectors are trained to
a mature budget under a common protocol?

**The three arms**, with per-epoch source counts verified identical in all 12 epochs
of all 9 executed arms:

| Arm | Normal-real | Defective-real | Synthetic | Effective defective |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| A `standard_real` | 992 (50.0%) | 992 (50.0%) | 0 | `0.500` |
| B `prevalence_matched_real` | 744 (37.5%) | 1,240 (62.5%) | 0 | `0.625` |
| C `gan_1500` | 744 (37.5%) | 744 (37.5%) | 496 (25.0%) | `0.625` |

Repeating two-batch (eight-slot) units at batch size 4:

```
A  [N D N D]  [N D N D]
B  [N D N D]  [N D D D]
C  [N D N S]  [N D D S]
```

**B and C are slot-for-slot identical except at batch position 3**, where B takes the
next real defective draw and C takes the next synthetic sample. `C − B` therefore
isolates synthetic *content* with prevalence held fixed. `A − B` is reported as a
secondary, **ungated** measurement of the pure prevalence effect.

**Immutable parameters.**

- **Seeds 45, 46, 47** — fresh. Seeds 42–44 were burned (they carry the G2.2 arms and
  the G2.3A analysis that informed this design), so reusing them would have made
  G2.3B a re-test of its own design data.
- **5,952 successful optimizer updates per arm** = 496 updates/epoch × 12 epochs at
  batch size 4, 1,984 slots/epoch. **Zero skipped updates; a skip is fatal.**
- Mature BF16 semantics reproduced exactly from the accepted baseline (§3.3).
- **Early stopping is monitor-only** — `early_stopping_would_have_triggered` is
  recorded per epoch and never acted on, preserving the equal-budget invariant.
- Checkpoints 1,000 and 2,000 are **refused by name**.

**Deterministic scheduling.** One common framework builds every arm's schedule; the
arm chooses only how many draws of each class it consumes and where those slots sit.
Draw streams are **arm-independent**: for each `(seed, epoch, class)` a single stream
is built to the longest length any arm needs, keyed
`experiment_version:seed:epoch:class` — **the key contains no arm**, so no arm can
perturb another's draws, and every arm consumes a *prefix* of the identical stream.
Identical initialization is enforced: the runner refuses to continue unless all three
arms report the same `initialization_sha256`.

**Threshold-selection rule**, precommitted and identical for every arm and seed,
applied to development validation only: train the full budget → reload the best
checkpoint (minimum validation total loss over 12 epochs) → sweep the fixed 99-point
grid `{0.01 … 0.99}` → select maximum validation global Dice, tie-broken by mean
defective-image Dice → pixel precision → smallest threshold. The runner refuses to
proceed if the sweep did not use the precommitted grid.

**Durability mechanisms** (`src/defectgen/training/g2_3b_protocol.py`,
`tests/test_g2_3b_durability.py`, 43 tests): atomic checkpoint writes, run-identity
verification, resume compatibility, completed-epoch and completed-arm protection,
state round-trip, LR restoration, and plan-unchanged assertions.

## 11. Engineering contributions

This is the part of the project that generalizes beyond the negative scientific
result.

### 11.1 Determinism and reproducibility

- Global seeding with `deterministic_algorithms: true`, `cudnn_benchmark: false`,
  worker seeding (`src/defectgen/training/reproducibility.py`).
- **Arm-independent draw streams** keyed `experiment_version:seed:epoch:class` — the
  key deliberately excludes the arm, so arms cannot perturb one another; each
  consumes a prefix of the identical stream.
- **Enforced identical initialization** within a seed via `initialization_sha256`
  comparison; the runner refuses to continue on mismatch.
- Deterministic schedules whose content hashes are precommitted and re-verified;
  `--mode plan` regenerates them byte-identically.

### 11.2 Content-hash integrity everywhere

Configs, schedules, manifests, plans, and checkpoints all carry SHA-256 content
hashes that runners compare at load time. Canonical *content* hashes are
distinguished from raw *file* hashes by design. Frozen checkpoints are re-hashed at
every run; wrong checkpoints are refused **by name**.

### 11.3 Atomic, resumable training

`atomic_torch_save` writes to a temporary path then `os.replace`s — a crash cannot
leave a half-written checkpoint. Checkpoints carry model, optimizer, scheduler,
scaler, RNG state, data epoch and position, counters, and last completed operation.
Resume verifies run identity and refuses to reopen a completed epoch or arm. CPU
tests prove uninterrupted and resumed training produce **identical parameter
hashes**.

### 11.4 Data-integrity and leakage controls

Manifest-only sample discovery (no directory globbing); per-file SHA-256 verification
across all 1,536 per-row synthetic files; `assert_permitted_split` admitting only
development `train`/`validation` and raising `OfficialTestAccessError` otherwise;
`train_g2_3b_utility.py` having **no** official-test mode at all; a test asserting
the script contains no quoted reserved-split literal.

> `scripts/train_g2_2_detector_utility.py --mode official-test`. It refuses to run

## 14. Challenges and debugging

### 14.1 Width-biased background sampling (F1.1)

**Problem.** Normal-background eligibility used a fixed `0.9` valid-fraction
threshold. Because KSDD2 widths vary 184–241 px, that threshold silently admitted
wide images and rejected narrow ones, biasing the synthetic background distribution
away from the true training distribution.
**Solution.** Audit all 1,772 normal training images, measure the actual native-valid
fraction distribution (min 0.71875, median 0.89453), and set the threshold to the
empirical minimum. Background inclusion went to 100% with no width bias, while
reflected padding remained ineligible for defect placement.
**Lesson.** A threshold that looks like a quality filter can be a distribution filter.

### 14.2 Border-contact semantics (F1.2)

**Problem.** A defect that genuinely ran off the edge of its source image could be
transformed and placed in the middle of a background, fabricating a geometry that
cannot physically occur — a truncated defect with no truncating edge.
**Solution.** Track explicit contact sides per template, transform them with the
flips, and require the placed defect to touch the corresponding native edges.
Non-border defects keep an 8-px native margin. Verified by
`accidental_contact_violations = 0`.

### 14.3 Placement asymmetry and impossible-state retries (F1.3 → F1.4)

**Problem.** Reflection padding was centred on the tensor rather than the native
window, and placement was validated against tensor edges instead of inclusive
native-valid bounds. Left/right sampling became asymmetric and the sampler burned
retries on states that were geometrically impossible. The F1.3 audit failed 17/1,000
samples with 243 empty compatibility pools.
**Solution.** Centre padding on the native window; evaluate placement against
inclusive native-valid bounds; index feasible flip and rounded-scale intervals
*before* sampling rather than rejecting after. Result: 1,000/1,000 success, 0 empty
pools, 0 retries. The audit now reports expected-versus-observed target-side counts
and horizontal-counterpart pool symmetry so the asymmetry cannot silently return.

### 14.4 FP16 divergence in the detector baseline

**Problem.** The E1 baseline (LR `1e-3`, FP16 + GradScaler, no clipping) produced
infinite unscaled gradient norms at epoch 1 batch 338 and epoch 3 batch 14, and died
during epoch-4 validation. Forward losses and logits were finite each time, so the
failure was not an obvious NaN in the forward pass.
**Diagnosis.** CPU inspection found every persisted tensor finite, and a read-only
precision diagnostic re-ran both checkpoints under FP16, BF16, and FP32 with finite
outputs and unchanged parameter hashes — **localizing the failure to the unsaved
epoch-4 optimizer state**, not to either checkpoint.
**Solution.** Switch to BF16 (wider exponent range, no loss scaling), drop LR to
`3e-4`, add gradient clipping at max norm 1.0, remove GradScaler, and disable
automatic fp32 retry so failures are loud. The replacement run completed
5,952/5,952 updates with zero anomalies.
**Lesson.** Precision failures can live in optimizer state rather than weights, and
"automatic retry" conveniences hide exactly the signal you need.

### 14.5 Generator residual saturation

**Problem.** The first gated smoke run stopped after **one** joint update: clamp
saturation `0.07349` exceeded the mandatory `0.05` gate. Verified genuine — 1,554 of
21,201 support pixels exceeded `[-1, 1]` while the input composite had none.
**Solution.** Redesign the output path: zero-initialize the final convolution so the
generator begins as an exact identity, and replace post-hoc clamping with
`range_aware_residual`, which bounds the residual by both the configured magnitude
and the pixel's actual remaining headroom to `±1`. Range violations became
*structurally impossible*: `output_range_violations = 0` over all 2,000 sustained
updates.
**Lesson.** Prefer constraints that make a violation unrepresentable over penalties
or clamps that clean it up afterwards.

### 14.6 Discriminator instability and a false-positive safety gate

**Problem A.** The discriminator was gradient-clipped on 70.48% of updates overall
and 98.33% over the final 60 steps — a strong instability signal.
**Solution A.** A single controlled ablation (clip `5 → 10`, LR `5e-5 → 2.5e-5`)
reduced clipping to 30.92% / 46.67% and reduced detector-statistic L2 distance from
genuine real (0.22863 → 0.15758). Even so, clipping remained at 91.69% in the final
sustained run — this was contained, not solved, and is reported as a limitation.

**Problem B.** The candidate stopped at joint step 197 on a canonical-gradient check.
**Diagnosis.** The gate itself was wrong: it counted nonzero RGB *scalar components*
instead of finite, nonzero RGB *vectors per canonical pixel*. The terminal update had
65,207 active pixels out of 65,208 — a rounding-level artifact, not a model failure.
**Solution B.** Correct the gate; the same checkpoint then resumed deterministically
and completed 200/200 updates with all four violation counters at zero.
**Lesson.** When a safety gate fires, verify the gate before blaming the model — and
verify the model before trusting the gate.

### 14.7 The methodological problem: evaluating synthetic augmentation

This was the hardest and most consequential problem, and it is a *reasoning* bug
rather than a code bug.

**Problem.** G2.2 compared a synthetic-augmented arm against a real-only control and
found a large apparent Dice gain (+0.084 mean over three seeds). It would have been
easy to report that as success. Two things were wrong:

1. **Prevalence confound.** Every synthetic sample is defective, so injecting 25%
   synthetic data into a 50/50 batch raised the arm's effective defective fraction to
   **0.625** while the control stayed at **0.500**. The comparison could not
   distinguish synthetic content from defect prevalence *even in principle*.
2. **Underconvergence.** Both arms trained for 2,000 updates — **33.6%** of the
   mature budget, ≈4.03 baseline epochs — with training loss still falling. The
   control's normal-image FPR was 0.5974–0.9265 against the converged baseline's
   0.0415. The "gain" was substantially a gain over a control that had not yet
   learned to stay quiet on normal images.

**Solution.** G2.3A quantified both problems using **zero** training — recomputing
prevalence from saved schedules and reproducing every G2.2 metric to a maximum
absolute delta of exactly `0.0` to prove it was measuring the same models. G2.3B then
corrected the design by construction: a third **prevalence-matched real control** at
0.625, a **mature 5,952-update budget**, **three fresh seeds** (45–47, since 42–44
were burned by the design analysis), precommitted per-arm operating-point selection,
and an **added threshold-independent PR-AUC criterion**. The whole gate was frozen in
a commit that precedes the training commit.

**Outcome.** The apparent gain disappeared. Mean Dice gain `−0.000196`, mean PR-AUC
gain `−0.002608` — approximately zero. The recall criterion that G2.2 failed now
passed comfortably, confirming the confound was real, but what replaced it was a
cleaner and more negative finding: **with prevalence matched, synthetic content
produced no aggregate quality gain.**

**Lesson.** The control defines what a result means. A two-arm synthetic-augmentation
comparison is structurally incapable of isolating synthetic content, and a
precommitted gate is what stops a promising-looking confounded number from becoming
a published claim.

   threshold 0.5 and **0.7983** at the swept 0.05, with normal-image FPR 0.0415,
   trained to completion with zero numerical anomalies.
3. **A technically sound generator.** 2,000 sustained joint updates with **zero**
   output-range, locality, invalid-gradient, and non-finite-gradient violations; all
   512 materialized samples passing every structural audit.
4. **Experimental hygiene that held under pressure.** Content hashes, precommitted
   protocols, frozen gates, fresh seeds, explicit schedules, and hard leakage guards
   were used consistently — and the guards were *exercised*, not merely declared:
   gates stopped runs (clamp saturation), a gate was found to be buggy and corrected,
   and a promising result was retracted after diagnosis.
5. **A rigorously diagnosed negative result.** G2.3A identified a confound that
   invalidated the earlier comparison while training nothing; G2.3B corrected it by
   construction and returned a clean, attributable answer.

### 15.2 What remains unproven

- **Whether a small true effect exists.** With three seeds and per-seed Dice gains
  spanning `−0.037` to `+0.019`, the experiment cannot distinguish a small positive
  effect from zero. It *can* and does exclude the large positive effect the
  precommitted gate required. **No significance test was performed**, so no claim of
  statistical significance or equivalence is available.
- **How much of the outcome is seed dispersion.** Seed 47 alone reverses the sign of
  the mean on Dice, IoU, and PR-AUC.
- **Whether a different configuration would succeed.** One synthetic ratio (25%), one
  GAN checkpoint (1,500), one generator formulation, one budget. Checkpoint 2,000 was
  never evaluated for utility; checkpoint 1,000 failed the G2.2 recall gate and was
  never carried into G2.3B. Nothing here supports the claim that more GAN steps or a
  different ratio would help.
- **Anything about official held-out test performance.** Every number in V1 is
  development-validation only, by design.
- **Whether the generator produces novel defect *information*** as opposed to
  plausible refinement of real-derived geometry. The architecture supports the
  "refinement" reading — identity initialization, residual magnitude capped at 0.25,
  exact outside-support preservation, losses localized to the support — but **no
  experiment isolated this**, so it is an architectural interpretation, not a
  measurement.
- **Generator output diversity.** No diversity metric was computed. There is no
  latent noise input, no style vector, and no diversity objective; variation comes
  only from template, background, placement, scale, and flips.

### 15.3 Do synthetic defects demonstrably improve downstream detector performance?

**No.** Under the corrected, prevalence-matched, mature-budget, three-seed experiment
with a gate frozen before training, GAN checkpoint 1,500 produced a mean Dice gain of
`−0.000196` and a mean PR-AUC gain of `−0.002608` against a matched real control, and
five of eight precommitted criteria failed. **No controlled multi-seed experiment in
V1 established a robust detector-performance improvement from GAN augmentation.**

Two distinctions matter and must be preserved:

- **"Utility not confirmed" is not "the generator failed technically."** V1 measured
  *downstream detector utility*, not generator quality. The generator trained cleanly
  and its outputs passed every structural audit.
- **"Not confirmed" is not "disproven."** The experiment excludes a large effect, not
  every effect.

### 15.4 Limitations

1. Defect geometry derives from only **232 real components**, varied by scale
   `[0.9, 1.1]` and flips. 512 synthetic samples are not 512 new pieces of
   information.
2. No explicit stochastic diversity mechanism in the generator; magnitude of this
   limitation unquantified.
3. Three seeds; no significance testing.
4. Development-validation only; official test sealed.
5. Single GPU, single OS. Bitwise cross-hardware reproduction is not claimed;
   schedule hashes, composition, and budgets are hardware-independent and do
   reproduce.
6. Discriminator gradient clipping remained at 91.69% in the sustained run —
   instability contained, not eliminated. The run also recorded warnings on monitor
   support containment and unequal contact-side sampling.
7. The successful F1.4 sampling audit is a local untracked artifact; the *tracked*
   audit is the failed F1.3 one.

### 15.5 What should and should not be claimed on a resume

**Safe to claim.** Building the end-to-end pipeline; the mask-conditioned residual
GAN architecture and localized loss suite; the 0.7777 Dice real-data baseline; the
deterministic/reproducible experimental infrastructure; the 448-test suite; the
diagnosis of the prevalence confound; the design and execution of the corrected
three-arm prevalence-matched experiment; and reporting a rigorous negative result
under a precommitted gate.

**Must not be claimed.** That the GAN improved detector performance; that synthetic
augmentation was validated; any official-test or held-out benchmark number; any
production, deployment, or factory use; statistical significance of any kind; or
that V2 exists.

**The negative result is a strength, not a liability, when framed correctly.**
Designing an experiment that could falsify your own hypothesis, detecting that your
own promising result was confounded, and reporting the corrected negative outcome is
a stronger signal of research maturity than a two-arm comparison showing a
plausible-looking gain.
